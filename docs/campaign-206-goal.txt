Design, from scratch, brick families that a Crazyflie 2.1 Brushless can carry with a passive magnet, hover over a structure with 16 to 40 mm of placement error, and drop onto it: the brick falls freely from about 11 mm and must seat by its own geometry. Read docs/requirements.md and src/brixzle/engine/gripper_spec.py. The score is Z (#222): the mean, over four placements, of Z = (seated - jammed) / N for the releases within 16 mm of the aim, measured on a grid. The four placements are a stack (a brick across two seated bricks) and the reaching bricks of courses 1, 2 and 3 of a back-first corbel (overhangs 1, 2 and 3), each dropped onto an ideally built corbel below it. If one brick stacks, many do; if each reach seats, the overhang builds. Z = 1, every release within 16 mm seating jam-free on all four, is the minimum worth printing; ties go to Z over 28 and 40 mm. Every design must meet the hard constraints in docs/requirements.md, including a magnet target, a mass of at most 25 g with its insert, the MVP gate (a stack of 3 and an overhang of 3 with ideal placement) and the screen (at least 32 of 40 random drops within 16 mm seated, 10 per placement). Try several genuinely different capture ideas before refining any one: the archive keeps the best design in each Z band, so a new idea competes in its own band. Results are simulated, uncalibrated contact. Do not copy designs from papers or other groups. With a free drop, every brick measured so far seats well on a full seat but tips when it lands reaching past an edge, in the sim and on the bench (a printed allround, maintainer 2026-11-27): a reaching brick that lands level and seats when dropped is the first problem to solve. Probe it with the screen (quick_check --quarters) and gridscore --placements corbel_reach,corbel_reach3 --no-jams before a full measurement.
